Site Survey — 82 Albano Street
Rex Tzen & Jan Kwek  ·  September 2026

The site as found

What the survey tells me

🌱  One corridor, not three rooms
The finding that shapes everything else
  1. The lot currently reads as a single passage from house to fence. It has the bones of three distinct areas — the deck and arbor at the house, growing space in the middle, a looser end at the back — but nothing edges them, so none of them reads as a place you would stop.
  2. On a lot 15 feet wide, the usable growing surface is vertical. The fence and the arbor are more square footage than the ground between them.
🍇  The arbor is underperforming, and it is fixable
Your best existing asset
  1. Grapes fruit on new wood. An arbor carried year to year without a hard winter cut makes shade and leaf rather than fruit — which matches what I saw. This is a pruning question, not a planting one.
  2. The same observation applies to the shade it casts: some of what reads as the shaded half of the lot is the arbor's own doing, and is therefore adjustable rather than fixed.
☀️  Sun is the scarce resource, not space
Where beds can and cannot go
  1. The open stretch is where anything fruiting has to live — fruit, vines, the sun-hungry vegetables. The shaded stretch is not wasted, but it is a different garden and should be planted as one.
  2. The common failure here is putting the vegetable bed in whichever corner happens to be free. On this lot that corner is the shaded one.
🌾  The back end is doing nothing, and it could do a lot
An observation, and an idea attached to it
  1. The far end is the least used and least overlooked part of the property. That is exactly the profile of a meadow strip — cut once a year, late, and less work than the same area kept as lawn.
  2. It is also not decoration. Fruit and squash both depend on pollinators, so the wild end and the productive middle are one system. That is the argument for it; the argument against is that a meadow reads untidy for two seasons before it reads intentional, and that is your call, not mine.

Not yet surveyed

Three measurements I could not take, and they decide where beds can physically go: the side width right to left, the door opening, and which way the roof drains. Plus the light reading above, which needs a second visit at a different hour before it is worth trusting.

What I would ask you next

WildnessHow loose is acceptable at the back? That answer sizes — or cancels — the meadow.
FoodWhat do you actually cook with? Twelve things you use beats thirty you admire.
HandsHow much do you want to do yourselves? It changes what I would recommend, not only who does it.
