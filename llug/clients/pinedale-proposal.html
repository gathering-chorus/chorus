A Garden Proposal for Anthe Kelley
19 Pinedale Road  ·  Six hours, this week

What I saw

Your lot runs long and narrow, and the light is the strongest thing about it — high southern sun down the west side all afternoon, a shaded courtyard in the middle, tree cover at the far end. That range means three different kinds of garden are possible here. But the paths don't connect the parts, so the yard reads as one overgrown space instead of three usable ones.

The structure is already there: the two-foot stone wall, an established vegetable bed, and real natives holding ground — milkweed, goldenrod, grape, raspberry, black chokeberry.

The work

🌱  Understand
Learn your space
  1. Identify what's there. We walk the cleared yard together and name every remaining plant — what it is, and what it wants.
  2. Update the map. A corrected drawing of the garden as it actually is, plants labeled, light marked.
🔨  Build & Change
Shape the garden
  1. Clear the paths. Open every route through the yard. First, because nothing else can be judged until you can get to it.
  2. Remove the invasives. Take out what is crowding the natives you already have. I'll show you each one before it goes.
  3. Cut back the catalpas. Bring light back to the beds below. Heavier structural pruning is better in winter when they're dormant — a follow-up if you want it.
🌻  Grow
Nurture abundance
  1. Start a winter compost. A working pile at the far end, so this season's material feeds next season's beds instead of leaving in bags.
  2. Recommend next steps. A short, prioritized list for spring. No obligation attached.

The day

All of it in one six-hour block this week, in this order — access first, then removal, then the compost while that material is still on the ground. You end the day with a yard you can walk, natives with room, light back on the beds, and a compost pile working through the winter.
