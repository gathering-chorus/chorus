Why it is a separate product

The meta model earning its keep

Chorus's steps rhyme with permaculture's design cycle, and that is not borrowing. Chorus is built on the permaculture principle set, so it converges on the same shape — two systems descending from the same principles land in the same place.

Which is exactly why the garden app must not inherit Chorus's steps. ValueStream is the type; Chorus is one instance and Light Life is another. Same meta model, different content, no obligation to match. If the model can only produce copies of Chorus, it is not a model — it is a template.

So Light Life takes the formal cycle from its own field, in the language its trade and its clients already use.

The stream

SADIMET · one property, one season, forever

Permaculture's own design process. The D is Decisions, not Design, because in this trade the whole engagement is design — that distinction is the literature's, not mine. Each step carries the permaculture principle under it, in Hemenway's wording from our own graph.

SSurvey

Walk it and draw it once per property

Walk the site with the owner. Listen, sketch, measure what you can reach. Leave them a hand-drawn map of their own space — which is what actually wins the work.

Observe Household · Property · SiteMap in the garden
AAnalysis

Read the light and the ground every season

Zones by how often a space is used; sectors by what arrives from outside — sun, wind, water, access. Identify what is growing, including what is invasive or prohibited in this state.

Connect Zone · Sector · Planting · Plant in the garden
DDecisions

Agree what to do, and what it trades for per engagement

A set of work items for one property, each with hours and the season it belongs in, and the exchange attached — cash at an hourly rate, or a trade with the value still named so both sides can weigh it.

Get a yield Proposal · WorkItem · Arrangement at the desk
IImplement

The visit per booking

The day itself. Tick off what actually got done, log the real hours, and capture what nobody predicted — a wasps nest, a tree worse than it looked, a bed that turned out to be shaded.

Use small-scale, intensive systems Visit · WorkItem · Observation in the garden
MMaintain

Tend through the season ongoing

Photographs and short notes pinned to a place on the map. Low effort, high value, and the only step whose evidence can only be gathered while the season is actually happening.

Catch and store energy and materials Observation · Planting in the garden
EEvaluate

What grew, what failed at season end

Review the season against what was proposed. The corrected map is the artifact — the drawing amended by a year of evidence, with the previous version kept.

Mistakes are tools for learning SiteMap · Observation · Planting at the desk
TTweak

Schedule next season winter

Small corrections and the standing work, each placed in the window it belongs to. Catalpas cut back while dormant. Bare-root fruit trees in early spring. Invasive removal after leaf drop. Timing is the craft; this is where it lives.

Collaborate with succession Recurrence · SeasonWindow · WorkItem at the desk
↺ The loop returns to A — Analysis, never to S. A property is surveyed once; every season after re-reads it. That difference is what turns a customer into a client.

Phone or desk

Where each step is actually performed

Four of the seven steps happen standing in a garden with dirty hands, often out of signal. Three happen sitting down. That split is the product's hardest constraint and should be decided now, not discovered later.

Phone, in the field

  1. Sketch, measure, photographS
  2. Identify plants, mark sun and accessA
  3. Tick items done, log hoursI
  4. Photo and note on a map pinM

Desk, between visits

  1. Write and send the proposalD
  2. Redraw the map, review the seasonE
  3. Schedule the recurring workT

Consequence: capture must work with no signal and sync later, one-handed, camera first. Anything a person has to type in a garden is a liability — the field steps get a photo, a pin, and at most one sentence.

The domains

Seven domains · classes, attributes, edges

Six domains are Jeff's list. Work is the addition: a proposal is a set of work items, a visit records which were done, a schedule says when they recur — without it, a proposal cannot become a visit record without retyping everything. Types are indicative; every edge names its target class and cardinality.

Where to start

One slice, and it is time-boxed

M — Maintain, in Jeff's own garden, this month.

Not Customer, not the proposal generator. Maintain is the only step whose evidence can only be gathered while a season is running, and it is September. Everything else can be built in January from notes; Maintain built in January is a guess about what a person wants to record standing in a garden with dirty hands.

It is also the smallest honest slice — a photo, a pin, a sentence — and it forces four classes to be real underneath it: Property, SiteMap, Planting and Observation.

  • Does a Visit exist without a Proposal? Jeff's own garden has no client. Either the edge is optional, or he is a household of his own business — the second keeps the model honest and is probably right.
  • Is the map an annotated photograph or real geometry? Photographing a hand drawing and pinning to it is days of work. Modelling geometry is months. The clients value the hand drawing.
  • Where does JurisdictionStatus come from? A state prohibited-plant list is public data, and loading it once would let the app say something no competitor's says.
  • Is Bed needed in the first slice, or can an Observation pin to a Zone? Fewer classes now, a migration later.
