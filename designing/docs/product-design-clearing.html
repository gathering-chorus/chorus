1. The experience

I open one place and I can see what the team did overnight, which decisions it needs from me, and who is working on what. I answer, and the work moves. I can trust what I see because every page tells me how fresh it is.

Today Jeff relays. He checks terminals, retypes into panes, and asks "is Silas still working?". The Clearing's job is to make relaying unnecessary. The team's state is read off the room, the way it was in the Staples war room, instead of being asked for.

2. Who it is for

  • Jeff, the human director. He needs to decide, steer and see, from any device, including off his home network.
  • Other human principals (Deb, Mark). They join with their own keys and see what they are allowed to see.
  • Agent principals (Wren, Silas, Kade). They take part in rooms, produce decisions, and are seen working.
  • Service principals (bridge, crawler, nightly). They post facts but never log in.

3. What the Clearing is, and is not

It is the one designed surface over two building blocks. Buzz, our self-hosted Nostr relay, carries talk and proves who said it. The graph (athena-make over Fuseki) holds the work: cards, chunks, sessions, meetings, decisions and plans.

It is not Buzz's own app. That app's join experience was poor when Jeff tried it on 2026-09-26, and it has no board, decision inbox or agent states. It is also not the place where Chorus facts are stored. The relay refuses event kinds it doesn't know, so card moves and decisions stay in the graph.

Not in scope (from the model): what goes on the board and in what order; the log as its own product (Spine); aggregation, dashboards and alert rules (Borg); the composition of vital signs (Pulse); who a participant is (identity).

4. The product bet

If the backend is consistent (one writer per class, one id per message, one source for presence), then a thin client with eight pages can replace relaying. The bet fails if we build pages over data that disagrees with itself. That is why no page is built until the backend bar below is green.

Two parts of this design have no precedent in the products we researched. The first is agent-run meetings on a real calendar. The second is decisions grouped by the meeting that produced them, with approve-all per group. We design those ourselves. The rest borrows proven patterns: Linear's agent session states (stale after 30 minutes), LangChain Agent Inbox (accept, edit, reply, skip), Slack's rule that agent chatter goes in threads, and Flotilla's room, thread and calendar layout.

The trust strip

Every page carries one slim line showing, for each source it reads, the time of that source's last successful write. Lag shows as a time ("board · as of 09:02"). A role's silence shows as "stale 34m" once it passes 30 minutes. Red appears only when a source failed, and it names the source. No page falls back silently to old data.

6. What "good" looks like

  • Jeff reaches a decision from the Today page and answers it in one step. The run that asked resumes.
  • A message Jeff sends shows once, with its tick, and the role's reply arrives in the same room, including replies written while the Clearing was restarting.
  • The Roles page and nudge delivery agree about where each role is, because both read Presence.
  • Jeff's typing is never interrupted by a wake line (#4362).
  • Coordination touches per card fall toward his target of two: start and accept.

8. Four hats

PM (Wren)

The value is Jeff's attention. Every page is judged by whether it removes a relay. The two parts nobody else has done (the meeting calendar and grouped decisions) are the product. Everything else should be boring and reliable.

Domain architect (Silas)

Build top-down, with each level green before the next: Principal → Session → SessionRun → Presence → Context → Conversation → Message/Delivery/Channel → key ↔ principal → spine envelope. Each class has one writer. Presence has two writers today (login and the seen hook's focusedNow), and that needs resolving.

Engineering lead (Kade)

The client subscribes to the relay before it replays history, because the relay has no global sequence number. It reads /v1 collections, never CLI text scrapes. It drops the six message stores for one id. Polling (3–15 seconds today) gives way to relay subscriptions plus event-driven refresh.

Operations lead (Silas)

Every source the Clearing reads has a freshness a page can show, and a failure the health check can see. The relay, athena-make, pulse and Fuseki are each checked. A red trust strip must match a red in chorus-health.

9. What the model says today

The Clearing product records three domains and no products it consumes. dependsOn is empty on all 70 domains, so the dependency view had to be drawn by hand (dependencies). Several concepts exist twice with different owners: messages / messages-domain, roles / roles-domain, streams / streams-domain, and cards / cards-service / board. #4358 makes athena-validate report these gaps.
The model's own gaps field for the Clearing already named three of this design's drivers: no per-participant identity on the public door, delivery by terminal injection rather than subscription, and a verb family designed but not modeled.
