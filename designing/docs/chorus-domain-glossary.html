Chorus Business Glossary — DRAFT

Wren, 2026-09-25, card #4306. Updated from the 05-28 draft (#3109) to match chorusconsulting.ai and the live model. It stays a draft until Jeff says it is right.

Two kinds of words live here. The business words are what a client hears: what Chorus Consulting is, what it offers, how it uses AI. The product words are what the team uses: the parts of Chorus and where each sits in the flow of work. Words the model already defines (classes and their fields) are not restated here. They live, generated from the model, at /athena/vocabulary.html.

The business — the words on chorusconsulting.ai

Chorus Consulting Jeff

Is
Jeff's technology practice for small businesses in the Boston area: someone who has done it at scale, working with a few clients at a time.
Promise
You run the business. Jeff makes the technology around it work for you. A few clients at a time, plain answers, and your data stays yours.
Not
Not a website shop, and not hosted software on hardware the client has to own. Both were in the April page and were dropped on 09-25 (a website is a commodity, and people don't want to buy and run a server).
Home
One of the streams of Bridwell Consulting LLC (DEC-059), beside Light Life Urban Gardens.

The offer

Understand
Learn how the business runs and identify where to make changes or invest.
Connect
Get the tools the client already pays for working together.
Plan
Write the plan: what to change, in what order, and what it costs.
Support
Stay on call, monthly, for whatever comes next.

How Jeff works

Listen
The whole picture before the details, often drawn on paper so both people look at the same thing.
Show
A small working example and a plain plan with costs before any commitment (DEC-060's "outside-in").
Build & stay
One change at a time, in steps the client can watch, then monthly support.

Engagement

Terms
The first conversation is free. One small first piece, priced up front. Progress shown as it happens. Monthly support, no long contracts. Everything made is the client's to keep.

How AI is used

The team
A small team of AI assistants: product (Wren), architecture (Silas), building (Kade).
Jeff decides
Nothing the AI makes reaches a client until Jeff has read it, checked it and put his name on it. The AI never talks to the client's customers or acts on their behalf.
Open
If AI helped make something, the client is told, including where it is weak. Client information goes into an AI tool only with the client's agreement.

Chorus Playbook Jeff

Is
The DPOR diagnostic: an outside read of a growing company's technology, team and spending, reported as Debt, Performance, Opportunity and Risk. It comes from the FastX Partners work (fall 2024, with Bridget Snell and Chris Grasso). Jeff kept the 8-section framework when that partnership ended and renamed it the Playbook (DEC-059).
Tie
It is the assessment craft Borg encodes: walk into an unknown system, see what is there, name what is wrong (Jeff, 06-12). The first run was on Chorus itself (/dpor-report.html).
Site
Not on chorusconsulting.ai (Jeff, 09-25: "keep it simple and vague for now"). It is here as connective tissue: how the practice, Borg and the Playbook relate.

Chorus, two meanings Jeff, 09-25

The team
Chorus the consulting team: Jeff with Wren, Silas and Kade. This is who a client works with, and the "Chorus" in Chorus Consulting.
The product
Chorus the product: the system that builds other systems, so people and AI can work together well. It remembers what was decided and why, tracks the work, and checks results before anything ships. It is the parent of the products below (Jeff, 06-08). A work in progress, used on Jeff's own businesses first.
