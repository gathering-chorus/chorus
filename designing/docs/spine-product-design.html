Spine — Product Design

Wren · 2026-09-01 (#4045). Origin: Jeff — "i need basics first — spine product design", after I wrote a service design with no product above it. Ingest IRI: chorus:doc-spine-product-design. Companion: Spine — Service Design (#4044), which describes how the log works; this doc says what the spine is for and what composes it.

The product in one line. The spine is the team's memory: an append-only record of everything that happened, and a catalogue of everything that can happen. Those are two layers, not one — and getting them modeled as two is the whole of this design.

1 · Identity

Chorus has eight modeled products today — Athena, Borg, Chorus, Clearing, Convergence, Loom, Pulse, Werk. The spine is not among them. It exists as two domains, both Wren's, both at Directing:

DomainPurpose (from the model)Vocabulary it claims
spineBe the system's audit trail — the structured event log of all activity and state changes.EmitContract, EventCategory, Vertebra
eventsMake the team's history observable as data: one event vocabulary every emitter shares.SpineEvent

Two domains with no product above them is why the spine's docs, routes and vocabulary cannot be walked from anything. This design makes the spine a Product and gives those two domains a home.

State at land (2026-09-02). The Product row is served with hasDomain events. The second edge, to the spine vocabulary domain, is not writable yet: the domain and the product share one IRI (chorus:spine is typed Domain in the ontology graph and Product in the instances graph), so the shape refuses the edge as a self-loop. One side has to be renamed before the composition is complete — the graph-identifier scheme card (#1772) is where that call lands. The events edge was also moved off Clearing, which had carried it by accident; a domain has exactly one parent product.

The naming hazard, settled. "Spine" already names the value stream — how work flows through the team (chorus-spine.html). This product is the event log. The value stream keeps the metaphor; the product owns the noun in the model. Both docs now cross-link.

2 · Promise

Three promises, in the order they matter:

  1. Nothing is lost. The log is append-only and never rotated (Jeff, 2026-07-23). Disk is cheaper than a forgotten month.
  2. Anything recorded is reachable. A question about last Tuesday is answerable at the speed of a query. This promise is currently broken — see §6.
  3. What can happen is enumerable. The set of event types is the set of things the system is able to do, browsable as data rather than grepped from a schema file.

4 · Structure — types and instances

Jeff's framing, 2026-09-01: "think of this like an eda — we have types of events and then many instances of each type." That is exactly right, and it is the structural decision this product turns on. The two layers have different sizes, different lifecycles, different stores, and therefore different homes.

Why instances are not materialized

Two reasons, both load-bearing:

  • Fit. A triplestore is a poor home for a growing, time-ordered, write-once stream. The log already is a perfect append-only store; duplicating it into triples buys nothing and costs a second source of truth.
  • Risk. Materializing millions of subjects into urn:chorus:instances puts the whole graph in the blast radius of a bad deploy — the #3587 wipe class, at a scale where recovery is not a restore-from-yesterday.

Instead the spine follows the compute-on-read ruling Jeff made for metrics on 2026-08-31: the log is the record; values are computed at read time. The SHACL shape still governs — a projected occurrence conforms to SpineEventShape exactly as a stored one would. What changes is where the bytes live, not what the contract is.

The join is what makes both layers useful. SpineEvent ofType EventType. Downward: a type row links to its recent occurrences and its counts, so the catalogue is alive rather than a list. Upward: an occurrence resolves its declared fields and its value-stream stage, so a raw line becomes self-describing.

5 · Composition

ComposesOwnerCarries
events domainWrenEventType, SpineEvent — the vocabulary and the stream
spine domainWrenEmitContract, EventCategory — the writer's contract and the classification. (Vertebra is DEPRECATED since #3697, superseded by ValueStreamStep; it stays claimed only until its 11 v1 rows are retired.)

logs (Silas, at Proving) is deliberately not part of this product. Logs is Loki aggregation and runtime observability — a consumer of the spine, not a constituent of it. Keeping that line clean is what stops "the spine" from becoming a synonym for "all telemetry."

Composition is unrepresentable today. The chorus product record carries no hasDomain edges at all, so no product currently states which domains compose it. This design fixes that for spine and restores chorus's edges in the same land.

Promise 2 is broken. Every reader takes a fixed 4 MB tail, which on today's traffic is the last 3 h 17 m — 0.25% of what is retained. Forty days are kept and three hours are reachable. Measured and detailed in the service design.
A generated route serving zero rows reads as working. /v1/spineevents is mounted, shaped, and empty. That is the hollow shape (#3734) at the API layer, and it is the direct consequence of modeling one class to be both a vocabulary and a stream.

10 · To-Be — path to close

  1. Make the product real (#4045) — the Product row, its two hasDomain edges, chorus's restored edges, and this doc served as a Document with a resolving href.
  2. Split the layers (#4046) — model EventType, load the 345 from the registry, serve /v1/eventtypes, and turn /v1/spineevents into a real projection with ofType joining both directions. The type leg has no dependencies and should ship first.
  3. Restore reach (#4044) — a day-to-offset index so a reader asks for a window instead of a byte count, which is also what makes the occurrence API able to answer anything older than this morning.
  4. Close the MCP correspondence — with types modeled, "every tool leaves an event" becomes a checkable claim: 57 tools against 345 types, holes reported in both directions. Not yet carded; it is the discussion Jeff asked for, not a ticket.
