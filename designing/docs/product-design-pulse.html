1. The experience

Jeff looks once and knows whether his team is working — without asking anyone, and without being lied to.

That is the whole product. Everything below exists to make that one sentence true, and the measure of the product is not uptime or latency: it is how many times in a day Jeff has to type "are you working?" On 2026-08-25 he asked six times, and each time the answer was already in the log.

2. Who it is for

JeffAt a glance, on a phone, often off the LAN. He is the only consumer whose time is irreplaceable, so a reading he cannot reach is a reading that does not exist.
The rolesMutual observation. A role should read a peer's state rather than message them to ask — the war-room rule (Jeff, 2026-08-25): "we definitely are not messaging each other to see if we are working or not."
AlertingA consumer, not an owner. Alerts read pulse; pulse never decides what an alert is allowed to say.

3. What Pulse is — and is not

Pulse is a product, not a domain. Jeff, 2026-08-25: "pulse is a product not a domain — maybe domain is memory (short term)." Until that day one IRI carried both chorus:Domain and chorus:Product, so /domains/pulse and /products/pulse answered differently about the same subject. The Domain typing was retired on #4010; the IRI and its 228 inbound references stayed.

Its domain is the short-term layer of Memory — what is true right now and decays — alongside long-term memory and knowledge (#4010, chorus:ShortTermMemory).

Pulse is a read-through cache with freshness, not an aggregator. Silas established this in the service design on 2026-04-21 and it remains the governing thesis: every section has its own producer and cadence; pulse reads, annotates with age, and exposes. It never decides what a consumer may see.

4. The product bet

Chorus already emits everything needed to answer "who is doing what" — the spine carries agent.activity every ten seconds with phase, tool, elapsed and session. The bet is that observation beats declaration: a state derived from what a role did cannot go stale the way a state a role promised to update does.

142 cards have been spent on the declaration side of that bet as of 2026-08-25, and the state field is still wrong when it matters. That is the evidence for the bet, not against it.

6. Boundaries

Pulse does not own cards, messages, alerts or events — those live in their own domains and pulse holds only the hot register over each. It does not persist: anything worth keeping belongs to long-term memory. And it does not decide identity — who a principal is belongs to the identity seam; pulse only reports what that principal did.

Known and unfixed as of 2026-08-25. The state field is wrong by construction (pulse.rs:162), role messages are a count rather than content, there is no relation between roles, and machine load is absent — so a reading taken while the box was at load 363 is indistinguishable from one taken at load 7. Each is stated in the service design with its measurement.
