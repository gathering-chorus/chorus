The Journey Story

Athena is navigated as journeys, not pages. Two journeys move work through it; one ladder moves attention through it. This doc names all three, grounded in the week they were lived rather than imagined.

Wren · 2026-08-05 · from Jeff's ideation thread of 2026-08-04/05 · companions: UI inventory & to-be tree · site map (39 screenshots + narratives) · design language: athena-flow-mock (#3378)

Why "journey" and not "site"

On 2026-08-05, Jeff walked the Chorus UI for ninety minutes and found four breaks: an instance page that ended the descent in three dashes, a domain page ordered against his reading direction, an ops view with no way to see the model, and a front door linking only three disconnected pages. None of these were rendering bugs — every page rendered. They were journey breaks: places where the question "where does this let me go next?" had no answer. His framing: "~ like jx or user journey in athena", and the diagnosis that pages weren't the problem: "its lack of integration and design coherence."

So the acceptance question for every Athena surface, from here on:

Does the journey continue from here?
Not "does the page render." Every surface must answer: where am I, what am I looking at, and where can I go next — up, down, or across. A page that renders but strands the reader fails review. The site coherence check enforces the mechanical half (responds + renders real content); Jeff's walk enforces the rest.

The rule at the cross-link: wherever a domain appears, the same model fold appears, drawing from the same generated endpoints — a shared component, not a copy (#3757). Two lenses, one substrate, visibly stitched. The failure mode is never having two views; it's forgetting they share one truth.

The two work journeys

Same six muscles, opposite weighting. The week of 2026-08-04/05 ran both back to back, which is why the examples below are receipts, not hypotheticals.

Building new — the work is authoring

  1. Shape — name the domain, its value-stream place, what it claims
  2. Model — classes and properties through the governed pen; the shape declares its instance home (ADR-051)
  3. Generate — owl-api mounts the surface the moment the shape lands; pages project
  4. Populate — instances flow in through the governed writer
  5. Prove — the serve proof: right graph, whole content, source-exclusive
  6. Reflect — the ERD shows the new domain's lines, or its honest isolation

Lived example — Values (#3759): chorus:Value has zero triples today. Beck states the five values and their principle edges; the work is authoring them onto proven rails. Almost no history to fight; the risk is modeling wrong, which is cheap to fix.

Rewriting — the work is negotiating with the past

  1. Observe — measure what exists and where it actually lives; never from memory
  2. Model the target — what stays, what goes, and why, recorded
  3. Migrate — move the keepers to their declared home
  4. Retire — staged, verified deletions with regression locks; tombstones, not silence
  5. Repoint — every consumer onto the generated surface; old paths retired in the same motion
  6. Prove the transition — the three-state proof, where the change from red to green is the evidence

Lived example — Principles (#3749): 29 messy instances across three graphs, three broken read paths, an API serving 2-of-29 for months. Ended as: 14 PC principles in one declared graph, 29 verified retirements, every consumer on one generated surface, provenance in every envelope. All six of the week's two-states bugs lived in this journey — every one was history disagreeing with intent.

The platform consequence Jeff named: Athena should know which journey you're on. A new-build card runs the left column; a rewrite card runs the right; the tooling (pen, deploy, proofs) is shared, but the sequence, the risks, and the evidence differ. The two-journeys distinction belongs in how cards are shaped, not just in this doc.

Why the front end lagged the back end

Jeff, closing the thread: "the front end ux/jx is less organized than the back end ax." The reason is structural, not neglect: the back end has three agents as continuous users — every verb and refusal got hammered into shape by constant use failing loudly. The front end's only user is Jeff, occasionally — so incoherence accumulated silently between walks. The remedy mirrors the cause: give the JX the forcing functions the AX has. The coherence check is the front end's refusal discipline; Jeff's review walk is its gemba, made a ceremony instead of an accident.

The plan

Chunk athena-jx, in order: this doc (#3758, the plan on paper) → the to-be tree reviewed by Jeff → #3761 (the entrance + shared chrome + nav spine, the flow mock as design language, the crawl as the negative proof) → #3757 (the model fold at ground level). The dispositions live in the inventory; corrections belong on #3761 as comments.
